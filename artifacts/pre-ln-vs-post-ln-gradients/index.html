Pre-LN vs post-LN: how big is the gradient?

Backprop through a LayerNorm multiplies the gradient by 1/σ, where σ is the rms of whatever that LayerNorm divides. Both stacks end in a LayerNorm. The difference is how big σ gets. Drag the depth and watch σ and the gradient move together.

The comparison comes from Xiong et al., On Layer Normalization in the Transformer Architecture (2020). They argue that at initialisation the gradient on the top layers is large for post-LN and about √L smaller for pre-LN, and use that to explain why post-LN needs learning-rate warmup and pre-LN can mostly skip it. Post-LN normalises after every block, so its LayerNorm only ever sees one block's worth of growth. Pre-LN never normalises the stream itself. Each block adds a roughly independent branch output, the variances add, and the rms climbs like √L.

Where the measurement and the paper part ways

  • The √L shrink is not uniform across pre-LN blocks. Only the top block sits behind a stream of size √L. Block l is shrunk by its own stream size, about √l, and block 1 is not shrunk at all. Going down the stack, the residual branches add gradient back at the same rate the stream grew going up.
  • Xiong et al.'s Theorem 1 is an upper bound on the last FFN layer. It states O(d√ln d) and O(d√(ln d / L)), not Θ. Its claim about the other layers is a preliminary analysis.
  • √L is the asymptote. The stream starts at rms 1 and each block adds less than one unit of variance, so at L = 64 the measured shrink is , not √64 = 8×.
Check yourself: what does GPT-2's residual init do to the first chart?

GPT-2 (Radford et al., 2019) scales the init of the residual layers by 1/√N, where N is the number of residual layers. With an attention and an FFN branch per block, that's 1/√(2L) on every residual-branch output projection. Decide what that does to the pre-LN curve before opening the answer.

Answer

There are 2L branch writes, each with variance scaled by 1/(2L), so their total stays O(1) however deep the stack is. The stream stays O(1), the final LayerNorm's 1/σ stays O(1), and the top-block shrink goes away. This one is reasoning, not something measured on this page.
