**The finding: neither ceiling is binding.**

Your modeled arithmetic intensity is 555–1033 FLOP/byte, all comfortably above the ridge of 381. That predicts compute-bound, so MFU should approach 100%. It's 19–40%. Meanwhile BW utilization is 11–24%. **Neither resource is saturated at any size.** For GDN to be bandwidth-bound it would have to move 4.3–17 MB per token against your modeled 0.5–2.0 MB — roughly 9× more.

So the gap is real traffic and real inefficiency your idealized model doesn't capture: unfused elementwise operations round-tripping activations through DRAM, low tile occupancy at $d_h{=}64$, and kernel launch overhead. Recall the standalone FlashAttention kernel hit 20 TFLOP/s, about 22% of matmul peak, all by itself — that alone accounts for much of attention's shortfall.

Two clean diagnoses fall out. **GDN's BW utilization is pinned at 11.5% across a 7.6× parameter range** — flat to within 0.7 points. Something size-independent is throttling it, which is the signature of per-kernel overhead in the chunked scan rather than any roofline limit. And **GDN's modeled intensity is *higher* than attention's at every size, yet it's slower everywhere** — its model is more wrong, which is exactly what many small unfused kernels look like.

**Slope decomposition** — the single fit of 0.68 hides three different mechanisms. The 60M→125M pair is a controlled depth-doubling at fixed $d{=}768$, which lets you solve for a fixed per-token cost:

| mixer | fixed µs/tok | per-layer µs/tok at $d{=}768$ |
|---|---|---|
| attn | 3.24 | 1.51 |
| gdn | 3.18 | 2.56 |

Doubling depth costs 1.81×, not 2×, because ~19% of the 60M step is the LM head and optimizer, which don't scale with $L$. Strip that fixed cost out and the per-layer time grows as $d^{0.84}$ from 512→768, then $d^{1.34}$ from 768→1024 (GDN: 0.96 then 1.20). Compute-bound would be $d^2$.

So the story is: **the sub-linear slope is a width effect, not a depth effect.** Wider layers are disproportionately cheaper because their matmuls have higher intensity and better tile utilization, and the exponent is climbing toward 2 as $d$ grows — which is the same fact as MFU rising from 27% to 40%. At $d{=}512$ you're paying for inefficiency; by $d{=}1024$ you're starting to pay for arithmetic.

**Two things I'd flag as spawned questions, not today's work.** GDN's memory overhead grows with depth (1.38× at $L{=}10$, 1.47× at $L{=}18$) while its parameter overhead stays flat at 1.17× — the chunked scan is saving more per layer than attention does. And FLOPs-per-token from the LM head falls from 29% at 30M to 10% at 250M, so "FLOPs per parameter" isn't constant across your sizes; any compute-matched comparison in D2 needs to account for that.

## The one I think you mean: the recurrent state update

Per head, per token, with state $S \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k \times d_v}$ and $d_k = d_v = d_h$, the gated delta rule is

$$S_t = \alpha_t\left(S_{t-1} - \beta_t\, k_t (S_{t-1}^\top k_t - v_t)^\top\right), \qquad o_t = S_t^\top q_t$$

Three matrix-vector contractions with $S$, each $d_h \times d_h$, each $2d_h^2$ FLOPs (one multiply + one add per element):

| Op | What it is | FLOPs |
|---|---|---|
| $S_{t-1}^\top k_t$ | read out what the state currently predicts for $k_t$ | $2d_h^2$ |
| $k_t(\cdot)^\top$ accumulated into $S$ | the rank-1 write | $2d_h^2$ |
| $S_t^\top q_t$ | query readout | $2d_h^2$ |

$$\Rightarrow 6d_h^2 \text{ per head per token}$$

The decay $\alpha_t \odot S_{t-1}$ is one FLOP per state element, $d_h^2$, and gets dropped from the count because it isn't a matmul. So $6d_h^2$, not $7d_h^2$, by convention.

**Careful with the symbol.** Summed over $H$ heads with $d_h = d/H$, the layer costs $6 H d_h^2 = 6\,d\,d_h$ per token — linear in $d$, not quadratic. So $6d^2$ is correct only when $d$ means *head* dimension. Writing it as $6d_{\text{model}}^2$ would overcount by $H$. This matters for your roofline table (D1/H1.2), so state which $d$ the header means.

Sanity check against softmax attention: $QK^\top$ and $AV$ cost $4dT$ per token across all heads. Crossover at $6dd_h = 4dT \Rightarrow T = 1.5 d_h$, i.e. around 192 tokens at $d_h = 128$. That's the right shape — linear attention wins on FLOPs almost immediately, which is why the interesting question on your box is bytes, not FLOPs.

## The other $6$, so you don't conflate them

$C \approx 6ND$ (params $\times$ tokens) is the *training* FLOP rule: $2$ forward $+ 4$ backward per parameter per token. Same numeral, unrelated derivation. If someone says "GDN costs $6d^2$" while discussing a training budget, they mean a $d\times d$ projection matrix under that rule, not the recurrence.

## For your Day 2 deliverable

Neither count is your bottleneck. At $d_h=128$, the state is $16{,}384$ elements $\times$ 2 bytes, read and written once per token per head — the recurrence is arithmetic-intensity $O(1)$, so on 273 GB/s you are bandwidth-bound at every size you'll train. That's the H1.2 story: report bytes/token, and let the FLOP count only justify why MFU is uninformative.

## 3. Gated DeltaNet (GDN) internal recurrence from the chunkwise kernel

**GDN update:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_t &= S_{t-1}\big(\alpha_t (I - \beta_t k_t k_t^{T})\big) + \beta_t v_t k_t^{T} \\
&= \alpha_t S_{t-1} - \alpha_t \beta_t S_{t-1} k_t k_t^{T} + \beta_t v_t k_t^{T} \\
&= \alpha_t S_{t-1} + \big(\beta_t v_t - \alpha_t \beta_t S_{t-1} k_t\big) k_t^{T} \quad \text{(fused)}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Forward pass:**

- $S_{t-1} k_t$ costs $2 d_k d_v$
- Outer product costs $2 d_k d_v$
- $o_t = S_t q_t$ costs $2 d_k d_v$
- **Total:** $6 d_k d_v$

## 1. You have the day's actual result and didn't report it

H1.2 (D1, directions doc) predicted: *"GDN-family layers will be closer to attention in tok/s than the FLOP ratio suggests."* Your own two tables falsify this, and in the more interesting direction:

| size | FLOP ratio (gdn/attn) | time ratio (µs/tok) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 30M | 1.04 | 1.50 |
| 60M | 1.07 | 1.56 |
| 125M | 1.08 | 1.62 |

GDN is **further** from attention in throughput than FLOPs predict, not closer, by roughly a factor of 1.5 in the discrepancy itself. That is the day's belief update and it should be the lead observation, with the ratio table in the post. The second half of H1.2 also fails: there is no "knee" — per-token time rises monotonically (12.9 → 48.6 µs for attention across the ladder), so cost is not flat in model size anywhere on this ladder.

## 2. The stated explanation is contradicted by your own numbers

> *"the crossover point ... is not met at our values of $T$, and hence GDN uses more compute and takes longer per token."*

The crossover analysis is correct, but it cannot carry that "hence." At $T=1024$, $D=512$, $d_v=128$: $T^\star = 4D + 3d_v = 2432$, so yes you're below it — but the resulting FLOP penalty is the 4–9% in the table above. The observed penalty is 50–62%. The analytic result explains about a tenth of the effect, and the post presents it as the cause.

Worse, the post then gives a second, incompatible explanation two paragraphs later (uncompiled elementwise ops) without noticing they conflict. The elementwise story is the one your data supports: near-equal FLOPs, large time gap, ratio roughly constant across sizes. Say that, and demote the crossover to what it actually is — a prediction about where FLOPs cross, which you are nowhere near testing at $T=1024$ and which the "next steps" $T=8192$ run is designed to check.

## 3. "All of them exceed the ridge point, so they are compute-bound" is a roofline misapplication

Two problems.

**Aggregation.** Roofline is a per-kernel model. Total time is $\sum_i t_i$ over kernels, and the operational intensity of a *composite* workload is not a roofline coordinate. If 60% of your time sits in elementwise kernels at $I \approx 0.5$ and 40% in GEMMs at $I \approx 2000$, the aggregate $F/B$ lands well above $I^\star$ while most of your wall clock is memory-bound. That is exactly the shape of a GDN block: L2 norms on q/k, sigmoid, softplus, SiLU×2, RMSNorm, output gating — each a full activation read plus write for a handful of FLOPs.

**Internal inconsistency.** If the workloads really were compute-bound at $I/I^\star \in [1.3, 2.4]$, you would expect MFU near the compute roof. You measure 18.7–40.2% MFU and 11–24% of bandwidth. Being far from *both* roofs is the model telling you its two parameters don't capture the bottleneck — that's the honest conclusion and it's a fine one to publish. The current sentence claims the opposite.

Related, and load-bearing: you report GDN as moving *fewer* bytes per token than attention (474 vs 602 kB/tok at 30M), which is what makes GDN's BW% half of attention's. Since $B$ is an analytic estimate, that number depends entirely on what you counted. If the attention estimate includes the $T \times T$ score matrix, it's wrong for a FlashAttention backend, which never writes scores to HBM. State in the post what $B$ counts (weights, activations, optimizer state, recompute) — otherwise the BW% column is uninterpretable and the attn/GDN comparison in that column may be inverted.

## 4. Parallel lines mean the opposite of what you wrote

> *"they are only a multiplicative factor apart ... Since this is size-independent, it may be some constant overhead that doesn't scale with width or depth."*

A constant *multiplicative* factor is precisely a cost that **does** scale with width and depth, in the same way the rest of the model does. A constant additive overhead would show as lines that converge as size grows (the fixed cost being amortized), which is not what you see. On a log-log throughput plot, "parallel" means equal exponent and different prefactor.

The correct reading: GDN's extra cost is proportional per-layer work — consistent with a per-layer elementwise tail, and inconsistent with a fixed setup/launch overhead. Same evidence, opposite conclusion, and it points at the right next experiment.

## 5. MFU denominator is non-standard and unstated

Reverse-engineering your table: $77{,}546 \times 334\,\text{MFLOP} = 25.9$ TFLOP/s, and $25.9/95.45 = 27.1\%$. So MFU is computed against your **measured** 95.45 TFLOP/s, not theoretical peak; BW% likewise against measured 222.37 GB/s.

That's a defensible choice — arguably a better one — but it's not what "MFU" means to a reader, and it makes your numbers incomparable to every other report, including the nanochat 5.6% reference in your own program doc. Your directions doc lists this as blind spot #5 with an explicit instruction: state the denominator in the table header. Do that, and either label the column "fraction of achieved peak" or report both. Same for the attention-backend microbenchmark — 5.07 ms at $(B{=}32, H{=}8, T{=}1024, d_h{=}64)$ is ~6.8 TFLOP/s if forward-only and ~24 TFLOP/s if forward+backward; the post doesn't say which, and the difference is the whole interpretation.

## 6. The analytic section and the measured section are never connected

Your derivation gives mixer-block FLOPs only: at 30M, $L(24D^2 + 6TD) = 94$ MFLOP/tok for attention. The measured table reports 334 MFLOP/tok — whole model, including MLPs and the output head. A reader who tries to check your work hits a 3.5× discrepancy with nothing in the post explaining it. One sentence fixes it: say the derivation is per-mixer-block and the table is end-to-end, and give the MLP/head share.

Also flag the asymmetry inside the derivation: attention is priced with $e_v = 1$ (QKVO all $D^2$) while GDN gets $e_v = 2$ plus a fourth projection for the output gate. So "the projection cost for GDN dominates, and is double that for attention" is partly a statement about the expansion ratio and the extra gate, not about the mixer's update rule. True as a fact about the blocks you're benchmarking, misleading as a fact about linear attention.
